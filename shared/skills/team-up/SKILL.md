# Team-up

Deliver one coherent result, not a collection of completed agent assignments.
Use parallel work to improve elapsed time and quality where dependencies permit.
The current parent owns the user relationship, scope, decisions, integration,
and delivery. Read `$create-subagents` at `../create-subagents/SKILL.md` for delegation mechanics;
this skill adds a whole-result contract, not a second orchestration hierarchy.

## Establish the shared outcome

Use the current task and original feedback to identify the intended result,
existing authority, protected work, and what would demonstrate completion.
Separate user decisions from suggestions, experiments, and agent interpretations.
Do not turn tentative feedback into a requirement or a team-up invocation into
authorization for new publication, production, Git, data, or configuration effects.

Set shared constraints and interfaces before dependent implementation: design
tokens, terminology, state/data contracts, or other actual points of contact.
Do not prescribe every implementation detail or require a new planning artifact.
Keep a small current record of ownership, dependencies, feedback coverage, and
integration evidence when needed for coordination. Ask only for a material
direction or authority gap; make reversible choices within the agreed task.

## Judgment and bounded execution

Keep overall direction, difficult tradeoffs and integration decisions with the
parent. Choose available models and supported reasoning for each contribution;
do not require a vendor or invent model identifiers. Preserve explicit user
model choices. A skill cannot switch the parent model or change configuration.

Use a fresh independent reviewer for the integrated result. Verify supported
delegation and model capabilities before dispatch. If independent review is
unavailable, complete authorized preparation and state the missing verification;
do not claim that the whole-result review occurred.

Divide work by unresolved judgment and dependencies, not line count. Give each
executor settled decisions, actual references, allowed discretion, ownership
and acceptance checks. Evaluate evidence and rework, not model reputation.

## Parallel making, continuous integration

Start independent contributions together once their shared assumptions are
clear. Keep useful work with the parent. Agent count follows the work, runtime
capacity, and integration cost; do not fragment tasks to fill available slots.
For dependencies, agree on a minimal contract or fixture before parallel work;
do not pretend blocked or overlapping work is independent.

Assign disjoint files, frames, or explicit single-writer ownership for shared
surfaces. The parent integrates by default; it may assign bounded integration
work but retains judgment over conflicts. Integrate useful increments
while other agents continue, and communicate contract changes to affected agents.
Do not allow outdated parallel work to overwrite a newer integrated decision.

Check cross-contribution behavior as it becomes available. Green unit checks or
individually attractive screens do not establish a coherent experience.
For an app, inspect transitions, shared state, terminology, and relevant failure
states as well as individual screens. Stop affected work when evidence conflicts;
resolve it before extending the same faulty assumption.

## One independent whole-result critic

Before dispatching the final critic, read
[the review contract](references/review-contract.md) and adapt it to this task.
The brief may be detailed; keep its judgment bounded and its evidence specific.
Give original user intent and constraints, not the makers' preferred verdict.
Independence requires sufficient task context, not deliberate context starvation.

The critic must not have made or integrated the assessed result. It reviews a
named, stable integrated version and reports to the parent. Use one designated
final independent critic, with specialist evidence where needed; do not ask it to
perform every possible audit. For substantial UI work, provide current rendered screens and interaction evidence to the reviewer. Its independent critic can be the final critic when its
role covers the agreed judgment; do not duplicate equivalent reviews. If that
role is narrower, use its findings as evidence for the whole-result critic.

The parent weighs findings against source evidence, resolves consequential
issues, and obtains a targeted recheck of changed behavior and affected seams.
Tie the final judgment to the version actually delivered. Reject unsupported
criticism with evidence rather than obeying scores or repairing imaginary defects.
Do not repeat the same failed check twice without a new hypothesis. At an actual
scope, access, or verification boundary, report the incomplete state honestly.

## Deliver the whole

Completion requires integrated contributions, proportionate verification, and
the independent critic's evidence-backed assessment of that integrated version.
An agent summary, an unexplained approval, or a high score is insufficient.
The parent remains responsible for the final decision and for explaining any
remaining limitation; a critic cannot waive the user's requirements.

Give the user one current entry point, what changed against their feedback, important
tradeoffs, checks performed, and unresolved or deliberately excluded work.
Distinguish locally implemented, tested, reviewed, and published. Do not end at
a plan or isolated contribution while authorized integration or repair remains.
Do not start other visible tasks or message another user-owned task unless asked.
